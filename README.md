## What it does

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only, no network) writes
a two-line explanation to `/output/entry.txt` — rain, explained
to a 10-year-old in one vivid sentence, then one honest sentence
about what the analogy maps and what it leaves out:

> Rain is the sky wringing out a giant sponge it left soaking all morning.
> The sponge part is right, because a cloud really does hold water the way a sponge does, but it leaves out the part where nothing squeezes it; the cloud just gets too heavy and lets go.

The entry is 257 characters long. Line 1 uses a single metaphor —
a giant sponge — a thing a 10-year-old can hold, joined to the sky
in a way they have not met before. Line 2 names both what the sponge
maps (a cloud holding water) and what it leaves out (nothing squeezes
it; the cloud just gets too heavy and lets go), instead of praising
the analogy. The second line keeps the picture honest: a vivid image
that leaves a 10-year-old with the wrong idea is worse than a plain
one that holds.
